Roundabout timing trainer

Each round starts with your car driving up the approach road. Pick your lane and indicator while you are still moving, the way you would in a real car. At the give-way line the only decision left is go or wait. On the roundabout, the signal button turns green when it is the right moment to indicate left.

    How a round runs
    • On the approach. Your car drives up the road towards the line, slowing as it gets there. Choose lane and indicator now. The bar under your instruction shows the road left and turns green once you are set.
    • Still clear as you arrive? Press go while you are moving and you carry on without stopping. That needs a bigger gap, because you commit early, but not stopping at a clear roundabout is the correct thing to do.
    • At the line. If you do not commit, the car stops. You can still change lane and signal, but it is marked as setting up too late.
    • On the roundabout. The button stays grey until you pass the exit before yours, then turns green: that is when the left signal goes on. Press it too soon and it is marked early.
    What the other cars are telling you
    • Approaching with left on: taking the first exit it reaches. With right on: going most of the way round. No signal: straight over.
    • On the roundabout with left on: it has passed the exit before yours and is leaving at the next one. If it leaves before it reaches your entry, it is not a threat.
    • On the roundabout with right on, or no signal: it is carrying on past your entry. Wait.

    Waiting for an empty roundabout is not the skill. Reading which cars will actually cross your entry is.

    The rules you are marked against
    • Give way to traffic from your right already on the roundabout. Keep moving if the way is clear.
    • Also hold back for a car waiting at the exit on your right (3 o'clock) that is signalling right or showing no signal. It is about to join and carry on round, straight across your entry.
    • 1st exit, turning left: left lane, signal left on approach, keep it on.
    • 2nd exit, straight ahead: left lane, no signal on approach, signal left after passing the 1st exit.
    • 3rd exit, turning right: right lane, signal right on approach, change to left after passing the 2nd exit.
    • Lane and signal go on in good time, on the approach, not at the line.
    • Sitting through a safe gap is undue hesitation and is a fault here, same as on test.

    This trains gap judgement and the signalling sequence. It is not a substitute for lessons with an approved driving instructor.
